COLUMN 1 — render ALL text at 7–8pt:
[SIENNA HEADER BAR] OGILVY'S LAWS THAT STILL RULE
Positioning is decision #1. "The results depend less on how we write than on how your product is positioned." Decide it before a word is written.
Make a large promise. "Promise, large promise, is the soul of an advertisement." A promise is a benefit, not a slogan — and the product must deliver it.
Build a brand image: a consistent personality, year after year, wins the largest share.
Find the Big Idea. "Unless your advertising is built on a BIG IDEA it will pass like a ship in the night." Big ideas are usually simple.
Make the product the hero. "If it doesn't sell, it isn't creative." Awards don't correlate with sales. [GREEN BEGINNER]

COLUMN 2 — render ALL text at 7–8pt:
[SIENNA HEADER BAR] THE EVIDENCE
The headline is 80 cents of your dollar — 5× as many read the headline as the body. Put the brand + promise in it.
The more you tell, the more you sell. Readership barely drops from 50→500 words. Answer every "why."
Don't bury the news. New is the easiest thing to sell — launch with a "Boom-Boom."
Respect the reader. "The consumer isn't a moron; she is your wife." "Nobody was ever bored into buying."
Substance over style: "What you say is more important than how you say it." Repeat your winners (readership rises up to ~5 repeats). [AMBER INTERMEDIATE]
[SIENNA HEADER BAR] WHAT AI CHANGES
The blank page is dead — infinite first drafts in seconds; you shift to judging & editing, where Ogilvy's taste lived.
The law still rules: only research + a big idea + a real promise make it SELL. Garbage brief → garbage copy, faster.
The loop: research → big idea → draft long → cut hard → test → repeat the winner. [AMBER INTERMEDIATE]

COLUMN 3 — render ALL text at 7–8pt:
[SIENNA HEADER BAR] SET UP CLAUDE
One Project per brand. Paste Ogilvy's rules + the brand brief + your swipe file into the Project instructions so every draft obeys them.
Keep a control file: your current best headline/copy, to beat.
Three surfaces, one craft: claude.ai (draft & iterate) · Cowork (run a campaign in the background, approve before publish) · Claude Code (bulk variants, landing pages, A/B at scale). [GREEN BEGINNER]
[SIENNA HEADER BAR] THE BRIEF (FILL BEFORE YOU PROMPT)
1. Product + how it's POSITIONED.
2. Audience: who, how they think, what they need.
3. The ONE promise.
4. Proof / specifics.
5. Voice (3 words + a sample).
6. Claims we must never make. [NAVY ADVANCED]

THREE equal columns, hairline 0.5pt rules. Burnt sienna (#8B3A1A) thin 18–20pt header bars, white bold all-caps. Body 7–8pt dense sans-serif #2a2a2a. ZERO padding. Paper grain. InDesign aesthetic. FOOTER espresso bar, white "WILL STEWART AI · Anti-AI Guru · empower-core.com".

FULL-WIDTH TAN SWIPE BOX under the title (terracotta left rule): OGILVY ADS THAT SOLD —
"At 60 miles an hour the loudest noise in this new Rolls-Royce comes from the electric clock." (specific > superlative) · "The man in the Hathaway shirt" — the eyepatch, pure story appeal, ran ~20 years · Dove "creams your skin while you wash" — one promise, repeated for decades · Flag the prospect: "MOTHERS…", "GOING TO EUROPE?" · News openers: "Introducing… / Announcing… / Now…"

COLUMN 1 — 7–8pt:
[SIENNA HEADER BAR] DO YOUR HOMEWORK
Ogilvy: "You cannot write copy unless you know who you're writing for, how that person thinks, and what they need." (He trained under Gallup.)
[SIENNA HEADER BAR] CLAUDE PROMPT — RESEARCH
"Act as a research analyst. From these reviews/call transcripts [paste], extract: (1) who the buyer is, (2) the exact words they use for the problem, (3) top 5 desires, (4) top 5 objections, (5) the single biggest promise we can honestly make. Cite the quotes you drew from." [AMBER INTERMEDIATE]
[SIENNA HEADER BAR] THE BIG IDEA
Ogilvy: "It takes a BIG IDEA to jolt the consumer out of his indifference. Big ideas are usually simple."
[SIENNA HEADER BAR] CLAUDE PROMPT — BIG IDEA
"Give me 10 big-idea angles for [product] aimed at [audience]. Each: a one-line concept + the single promise it dramatizes + why it jolts. Rank by promise strength. No puns. Then expand the top 2." [NAVY ADVANCED]

COLUMN 2 — 7–8pt:
[SIENNA HEADER BAR] HEADLINES (80% OF THE DOLLAR)
Ogilvy: "If you don't sell in the headline you've wasted 80% of your money." Benefit + brand + genuine news; long beats short. The two most powerful words: "FREE" and "NEW." Headlines in quotation marks lift recall ~28%.
Model: "At 60 miles an hour the loudest noise in this new Rolls-Royce comes from the electric clock."
[SIENNA HEADER BAR] CLAUDE PROMPT — HEADLINES
"Write 20 headlines for [product]. Rules: lead with the #1 benefit; include the brand; work in genuine news where true; use 'new'/'free' only when honest; 8–12 words; no wordplay. Then score each 1–10 on clarity + promise and give me the best 3 with why." [AMBER INTERMEDIATE]

COLUMN 3 — 7–8pt:
[SIENNA HEADER BAR] BODY COPY + PROBLEM/SOLUTION
Ogilvy: "The more you tell, the more you sell." Be informative; answer every "why." For problem-solution: set up a problem the consumer recognizes, then prove your product solves it — don't cheat.
[SIENNA HEADER BAR] CLAUDE PROMPT — BODY COPY
"Write 350–500 words of body copy for [product]. Open with the promise; name the problem in the customer's own words; give specifics + proof; answer why-it-matters / why-you / why-now; one clear CTA. Voice: [sample]. Then cut it 20% without losing a fact." [NAVY ADVANCED]
[SIENNA HEADER BAR] ORDER OF OPERATIONS [GREEN BEGINNER]
Research → big idea → headline → body → cut → test. Never skip research; never run a headline with no promise.

CRITICAL RENDERING RULES: Body 7–8pt. Thin 18–20pt header bars. Hairline 0.5pt dividers. Zero padding. Inline level badges. InDesign print-shop look — not an infographic. Spell every word exactly as written.

COLUMN 1 — 7–8pt:
[SIENNA HEADER BAR] TEST LIKE OGILVY
Ogilvy: "If it doesn't sell, it isn't creative." "Never stop testing, and your advertising will never stop improving." Direct response was his "first love and secret weapon" — because it measures. No correlation between awards and sales. Repeat your winners.
[SIENNA HEADER BAR] CLAUDE PROMPT — A/B
"Turn this winning ad into 10 headline variants and 3 CTA variants as a table ready for A/B testing; keep the promise identical, vary only the hook." Keep the control; only swap when a variant beats it on the metric. [NAVY ADVANCED]

COLUMN 2 — 7–8pt:
[SIENNA HEADER BAR] THE 3 CLAUDE SURFACES
[2-COL TABLE, tight] Surface | Use it for
claude.ai | research, big ideas, drafting, rewriting to voice
Claude Cowork | run a whole campaign in the background (emails, posts, landing copy); approve before publish
Claude Code | bulk-generate variants, build & A/B landing pages, personalize at scale, analyze results
[SIENNA HEADER BAR] THE MOVE [AMBER INTERMEDIATE]
Ideate in claude.ai → productionize in Code → operate in Cowork.

COLUMN 3 — 7–8pt:
[SIENNA HEADER BAR] OGILVY'S DON'TS
Don't: bury the news · talk down to the reader · be a bore · chase clever over selling · skip the research · run a headline with no promise.
[SIENNA HEADER BAR] SHIP-IT CHECKLIST [GREEN BEGINNER]
Is it positioned? One large promise? Benefit in the headline? Specific + proven? Does it answer "why"? Would you let your family read it?
[SIENNA HEADER BAR] WORKED EXAMPLE — FULL CHAIN [NAVY ADVANCED]
"You are a direct-response copywriter in the Ogilvy tradition. Brief: [paste]. 1) State the ONE promise. 2) Give 3 big ideas. 3) Write 10 benefit-led headlines (brand + promise, 8–12 words). 4) Write 350-word body that answers every 'why' and ends in one CTA. 5) Give 3 A/B variants of the headline. Obey the brief; invent no facts."
